# Unit 2: Choosing a Historical Figure — Virtual Lesson
**Pathway:** Archive Builder
**Duration:** 75 min
**Materials:** Internet Archive browser tab, corpus inventory spreadsheet template (shared via link), screen share capability for demos

## Agenda
| Time | Activity |
|------|----------|
| 0:00–0:10 | Check-in: which figure did you pick, and where did you first look? |
| 0:10–0:35 | Presentation: navigating Internet Archive + evaluating formats |
| 0:35–1:00 | Breakout: live corpus browse and inventory start |
| 1:00–1:15 | Debrief: what did you find, what concerns came up? |

## Slide Outline

**Slide 1: What the Builder Does in Unit 2**
- Your job is not to evaluate whether the figure is "worthy" — that's already decided
- Your job is to answer: is there enough usable text to build with?
- "Usable" = downloadable as .txt or _djvu.txt, publication year pre-1931, OCR quality 2 or above
- You will produce a corpus inventory spreadsheet, not a proposal document

**Slide 2: Internet Archive Navigation**
- Search for figure name, filter to "Texts"
- Look for a dedicated collection AND individual scattered items
- The item detail page: find the "Download Options" box on the right side
- Click through to see all available formats before downloading anything
- Show how to identify _djvu.txt files in the file list (filename ends in _djvu.txt)

**Slide 3: Format Hierarchy — What to Download**
- First choice: .txt — clean, ready to work with
- Second choice: _djvu.txt — rename it, it works the same way
- Last resort: .pdf — only if no text alternative exists, flag it for extra work
- What to avoid: .epub, .mobi, .xml — contain text but introduce parsing complexity
- Rule: if you have both .txt and .pdf for the same work, always use .txt

**Slide 4: OCR Quality Rating**
- Live demo: open a .txt file in a browser text view on Internet Archive
- Read 3 paragraphs aloud to the group — class rates the quality together
- Quality 3: clean, readable, publishable quality
- Quality 2: readable, some errors, usable
- Quality 1: garbled, large chunks unreadable — flag for decision
- Show a real quality-1 example so participants know what "bad" looks like

**Slide 5: The Corpus Inventory Spreadsheet**
- Share the template: filename | title | year | source_url | format | ocr_quality | notes
- Why each column matters downstream: filename → chunk script, year → metadata, source_url → provenance
- Fill it as you go — don't try to reconstruct it afterward
- This spreadsheet is a deliverable, not just bookkeeping

## Breakout Activity (25 min)
**Solo work with check-ins, groups of 2–3 for accountability**

Each participant browses Internet Archive for their figure during the breakout. Tasks:
1. Find all available texts for your figure (10 min) — search both the main collection and scattered items
2. Download 3 .txt or _djvu.txt files (5 min)
3. Spot-check OCR quality on each and add to the inventory spreadsheet (10 min)

Groups of 2–3 check in with each other at the 15-minute mark: "What did you find? Any format problems? Any quality surprises?"

Share-back format: each participant reports one finding — either a text they're excited to include or a problem they encountered.

## Debrief Questions
1. Did anyone find a text they wanted but couldn't get as .txt — only PDF? What will you do?
2. How did OCR quality vary across your files? Were certain types of works (books vs. magazines vs. pamphlets) consistently better or worse?
3. What would make the inventory spreadsheet more useful? What columns would you add?

## Facilitator Notes
- Internet Archive can be slow — have participants open the site before the session starts
- Some figures will have very few .txt files (only PDFs) — this is a real constraint worth discussing openly rather than pretending away
- Participants often underestimate how important the inventory spreadsheet is — emphasize that the filename column feeds directly into the chunk script's file processing
- _djvu.txt files confuse almost everyone the first time — demo the rename process explicitly so it's not a mystery later
