# Unit 2: Choosing a Historical Figure — In-Person Lesson
**Pathway:** Archive Builder
**Duration:** 75 min
**Room setup:** Laptop per participant (or pairs), tables arranged for easy collaboration, projector for demos, whiteboard for live notes
**Materials:** Printed corpus inventory spreadsheet template (one per person), sticky notes, Internet Archive accessible on all machines

## Agenda
| Time | Activity |
|------|----------|
| 0:00–0:10 | Warm-up: "What's your figure and where will you look?" |
| 0:10–0:30 | Facilitated presentation: Internet Archive navigation and format evaluation |
| 0:30–1:00 | Workshop: live corpus browse, download, and inventory build |
| 1:00–1:15 | Gallery share: spreadsheet snapshots and quality reports |

## Warm-Up Activity

**"What's your figure and where will you look?" (10 min)**

Each participant introduces their figure and names one place they plan to look for texts — Internet Archive, Project Gutenberg, HathiTrust, Library of Congress digital collections, or somewhere else. Facilitator writes all the sources on the whiteboard as participants call them out. At the end, circle the sources that multiple people mentioned and note which ones the group has experience with. This surfaces collective knowledge and flags research approaches the group can share.

## Facilitated Presentation

**Internet Archive navigation + format evaluation (20 min)**

**Part 1: Navigating to a figure's collection (8 min)**

Live demo on projector. Facilitator searches for "W.E.B. Du Bois" on Internet Archive as a model, then replicates the same search for a participant's figure. Walk through:
- Filtering by "Texts" in the left sidebar
- The difference between a curated collection page and individual item pages
- The "Download Options" box on an item detail page — what each format icon means
- How to expand the full file list to see all available formats (some items have more options than the icons show)
- The DJVU format: what it is, why it exists, how to identify _djvu.txt files in the list

**Part 2: OCR quality in the real world (6 min)**

Open three different .txt files from Internet Archive — prepared in advance — representing quality levels 1, 2, and 3. Read 2–3 sentences from each aloud. Ask the room to rate them before you tell them your rating. Key points:
- Quality 1 looks worse than you might expect — not just typos but incoherent fragments
- Quality 2 is very workable — the meaning is clear even if individual words are misspelled
- Quality 3 is the exception rather than the rule for pre-1920 texts

**Part 3: The inventory spreadsheet (6 min)**

Walk through the printed template column by column. Explain why each field matters downstream: the `filename` column feeds into the chunk script, `year` feeds into metadata display, `source_url` is your provenance record. Emphasize: fill it as you go. Reconstructing metadata after the fact is painful.

## Workshop Activity

**Live corpus browse and inventory build (30 min)**

Participants work on their own machines. Three-phase structure:

**Phase 1 (10 min): Browse and catalog**
Search for your figure on Internet Archive. Find as many texts as you can. Don't download yet — just add rows to your inventory spreadsheet for everything you find. Get at least 8–10 candidate texts into the spreadsheet before you start downloading.

**Phase 2 (10 min): Download and spot-check**
Download your 5 highest-priority texts (based on what you think will be most useful for your archive). Open each one and rate OCR quality 1–3. Update your spreadsheet.

**Phase 3 (10 min): Flag decisions**
Review your spreadsheet. Mark any files that need a decision: PDFs you couldn't find .txt alternatives for, files with quality-1 ratings, texts that might be post-1931. Write a one-sentence note for each flagged item explaining what decision is needed.

Facilitator circulates during all three phases to troubleshoot Internet Archive navigation issues and help participants identify quality problems.

## Synthesis / Gallery Share

**Spreadsheet snapshots and quality reports (15 min)**

Each participant either projects their spreadsheet or passes it to their neighbor to review. Quick round: each person states:
1. How many texts did you find? What formats?
2. What was the range of OCR quality you saw?
3. What's your biggest concern going into the next unit?

Facilitator writes the concerns on the whiteboard. Common themes: not enough .txt files, one or two critical texts only available as PDF, uncertainty about a publication date. Briefly address each theme — not to solve it but to name it as a known challenge the curriculum will address.

## Facilitator Notes
- Internet Archive can be slow or have intermittent outages — have a backup plan (pre-downloaded files shared via USB or cloud folder) for the download phase
- Participants often don't read the text during spot-checking — they just look at it. Require them to read at least 3 sentences aloud to themselves before rating
- The Phase 1/2/3 structure of the workshop is important — participants who jump to downloading without cataloging first lose track of what they have
- If anyone's figure has a very sparse Internet Archive presence, pair them with someone whose figure has a rich collection so they can learn the process before tackling their own harder case
