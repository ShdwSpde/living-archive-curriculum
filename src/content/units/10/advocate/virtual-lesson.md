**Pathway:** Archive Advocate

**Materials:** Slides, cost spreadsheet template (shared in chat), Anthropic pricing page open in browser

## Slide Outline

**Slide 1: The Two Questions Funders Are Really Asking**
- "What does it cost?" = two separate questions: build cost and operating cost
- Build cost: mostly labor, infrastructure is near-zero
- Operating cost: dominated by LLM API — 80%+ of ongoing expense
- Why this matters: you can write two different budget lines with different justifications

**Slide 2: The Cost Per Query Table**
- Table: Claude Sonnet ($0.030), Claude Haiku ($0.008), GPT-4o-mini ($0.0014)
- Monthly projections at hobby / moderate / growth traffic
- Key insight: at moderate academic use, you're talking $90–$195/month — less than many database subscriptions
- Live demo: show Anthropic's pricing page and walk through the math

**Slide 3: The Went Viral Scenario**
- What viral traffic looks like: 5,000 visitors in 24 hours, 3 queries each = $450 in one day
- Safeguards: rate limiting, spending alerts, model switching
- The advocate's job: ask "what happens when X?" before launch
- Questions to ask your technical team (show the four questions from the reading)

**Slide 4: Three Sustainability Models**
- Endowment: durable, requires upfront fundraising, best for permanent archives
- Institutional subsidy: lowest friction, most common for established institutions
- Grant renewal: most common for new projects, introduces discontinuity risk
- Combination strategies: how most archives actually survive Year 3+

**Slide 5: Writing the Budget Narrative**
- Funders want to see that you've thought through sustainability beyond the grant period
- Required elements: Year 1 setup, Year 2–3 operations, Year 3 transition plan
- Common mistake: not specifying who pays after the grant ends
- The "sustaining home" question: which institution commits to the operating cost?

## Breakout Activity (25 min)
**Groups of 3–4**

Each group receives the cost spreadsheet template (shared in meeting chat). Using a hypothetical archive — or a real one someone in the group is developing — work through:

1. Choose your traffic scenario: conservative, moderate, or ambitious
2. Calculate monthly LLM cost at two model tiers (Haiku and Sonnet)
3. Draft a two-paragraph budget narrative that: (a) justifies the Year 1 build cost, and (b) explains the sustainability plan for Year 3 and beyond
4. Identify which sustainability model(s) you're relying on and what commitment you'd need from an institution before launching

Groups report back with their monthly cost estimate and one sentence summarizing their Year 3 sustainability plan.

## Debrief Questions
1. What sustainability model seemed most realistic for your institution? What would have to change to make the endowment model viable?
2. How do you explain to a funder that 80% of your operating cost is paying for AI without making the project sound fragile?
3. What's the institutional commitment you'd want in writing before you launch? Who at your institution has the authority to make that commitment?

## Facilitator Notes
- The cost numbers in this unit will change as AI pricing evolves. Before teaching this unit, check current Anthropic and OpenAI pricing pages and update the table if needed.
- Some advocates feel anxious about financial modeling. Emphasize: you don't need to do this math in your head during a funder meeting. The spreadsheet does it for you. The skill is knowing which scenario to use.
- The "went viral" scenario often prompts anxiety. Redirect: the safeguards exist precisely so this scenario doesn't become a crisis. The question to ask is not "what if it happens" but "have we built the safeguards."
- Encourage groups to name a specific institutional home — not "the library" generically, but a specific budget line or named unit. Specificity makes sustainability plans credible.
