# Unit 1: Why Living Archives — Virtual Lesson
**Pathway:** Archive Advocate
**Duration:** 75 min
**Materials:** Slides, async page, Du Bois archive open in browser

## Agenda
| Time | Activity |
|------|----------|
| 0:00–0:10 | Check-in: one word for what surprised you about the Du Bois archive |
| 0:10–0:35 | Presentation: Living Archives in Context |
| 0:35–1:00 | Breakout: What figure would you archive? |
| 1:00–1:15 | Full group debrief |

## Slide Outline

**Slide 1: The Gap in Digital Scholarship**
- Static digital libraries: search by keyword, browse by document
- The gap: synthesis across a body of work, interpretive questions
- Living archives fill this gap

**Slide 2: How It Works (No Jargon)**
- Show the Du Bois archive — live demo
- Ask: "What was Du Bois's argument about double consciousness?" → show the answer + citations
- The magic: it found relevant passages across multiple texts, synthesized, cited

**Slide 3: RAG vs. Fine-tuning — The Decision Institutions Make**
- Two-column visual: RAG on left, Fine-tuning on right
- RAG: citable, updatable, cheap to build, $5–50/month to run
- Fine-tuning: stylistic mimicry, not scholarship — not right for archives
- Key message: RAG is not just technically better — it's *epistemically* better for scholarly use

**Slide 4: What Makes a Good Living Archive?**
- Large body of public-domain written work
- Questions scholars actually want to ask
- Curatorial decisions: what to include, how to frame, who to credit
- Living archive is an *interpretive act*, not just a tech deployment

**Slide 5: Institutional Questions**
- Who maintains it? Who updates the corpus?
- What's the cost per month at various traffic levels?
- How does it relate to existing digital collections?
- What does success look like in year 1, year 3?

## Breakout Activity (25 min)
**Groups of 3–4**

Prompt: Each person shares the figure they're considering archiving. For each figure, the group asks:
1. What kinds of questions would scholars want to ask?
2. What's in the public domain? How much of the corpus is accessible?
3. What institution would steward this? What's their stake?

Each group nominates the strongest candidate figure and reports back with a 60-second pitch.

## Debrief Questions
1. What made one figure stand out as a strong candidate?
2. What's one institutional question you'd need answered before proposing an archive?
3. What's the difference between a living archive and a chatbot? (Push for nuance here.)
