# Archive Advocate Pathway — 2-Week Schedule
**Pathway:** Archive Advocate
**Duration:** 2 weeks
**Sessions:** 6 (3 per week, ~75 min each)
**Final Deliverable:** Annotated corpus proposal

## Overview
This intensive introduces advocates — librarians, digital humanists, faculty, grant writers, and program officers — to the case for living archives. Over 6 sessions covering Units 1–6, participants move from understanding what a living archive is, to selecting a figure, to evaluating corpus quality and institutional feasibility. The final deliverable is a fundable corpus proposal document.

## Week-by-Week Schedule

| Day | Unit | Topic | Async Prep | Session Goal |
|-----|------|-------|------------|--------------|
| Day 1 | Unit 1 | Why Living Archives | Read Unit 1 async (30–45 min): what a living archive is, RAG vs. fine-tuning, browse the Du Bois demo | Understand the living archive concept; articulate the difference between RAG and fine-tuning to a non-technical audience |
| Day 2 | Unit 2 | Choosing a Historical Figure | Read Unit 2 async (30–45 min): figure selection criteria, corpus availability, institutional fit | Apply the selection framework to 2–3 candidate figures; narrow to one with institutional support |
| Day 3 | Unit 3 | Sourcing the Corpus | Read Unit 3 async (30–45 min): Internet Archive, HathiTrust, copyright and public domain, OCR quality | Evaluate corpus availability for your figure; identify 5–8 high-priority sources |
| Day 4 | Unit 4 | Text Quality & Preparation | Read Unit 4 async (30–45 min): what makes a good corpus, OCR noise, chapter structure, metadata fields | Assess quality of your corpus sources; identify which texts are ready to use and which need remediation |
| Day 5 | Unit 5 | Embedding & AI | Read Unit 5 async (30–45 min): what embeddings are, how RAG retrieval works, what vectors mean for scholars | Explain the embedding process to a funder or administrator without technical jargon |
| Day 6 | Unit 6 | Database Design | Read Unit 6 async (30–45 min): Supabase schema, chunk table structure, what a "document chunk" is | Understand what the database holds and why; describe the schema in a grant narrative |

## Final Deliverable

**Annotated Corpus Proposal** — a 4–6 page document with the following components:

1. **Figure biography** (1–2 paragraphs): who the figure is, why they matter, why now
2. **Purpose statement** (1 paragraph): what research questions the archive will answer, who the audience is
3. **Three research questions** the archive should be able to answer, with a note on why each is difficult to answer with existing tools
4. **Corpus inventory** (table format): title, source URL, date range, estimated pages, public domain status, OCR quality rating (1–5), priority tier (must-have / nice-to-have)
5. **Institutional home and maintenance plan** (1 paragraph): which unit will host the archive, who is responsible for updates, how often the corpus will be reviewed
6. **Cost estimate** (1 paragraph + table): one-time build cost, monthly operating cost at 3 traffic scenarios (low/moderate/high), proposed sustainability model

## Assessment Criteria
- **Corpus specificity**: The inventory includes at least 8 sources with accurate public domain assessments and realistic OCR quality ratings
- **Purpose clarity**: The three research questions are answerable by a RAG archive and not easily addressed by existing keyword-search tools
- **Institutional grounding**: The maintenance plan names specific roles and units, not generic commitments
- **Cost realism**: The cost estimate uses the unit benchmarks correctly and includes a credible sustainability model
