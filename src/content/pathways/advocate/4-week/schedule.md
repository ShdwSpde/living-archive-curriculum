# Archive Advocate Pathway — 4-Week Schedule
**Pathway:** Archive Advocate
**Duration:** 4 weeks
**Sessions:** 12 (3 per week, ~75 min each)
**Final Deliverable:** Full archive proposal + funder pitch deck

## Overview
The 4-week advocate pathway takes participants through all nine curriculum units plus three workshop sessions devoted to proposal writing, pitch preparation, and final presentations. Participants leave with both a written proposal and a presentation-ready pitch they could deliver to a dean, program officer, or development committee.

## Week-by-Week Schedule

| Day | Unit | Topic | Async Prep | Session Goal |
|-----|------|-------|------------|--------------|
| Day 1 | Unit 1 | Why Living Archives | Read Unit 1 async: what a living archive is, RAG vs. fine-tuning, browse the Du Bois demo | Understand the living archive concept; articulate the case to a non-technical audience |
| Day 2 | Unit 2 | Choosing a Historical Figure | Read Unit 2 async: figure selection criteria, corpus availability, institutional fit | Apply the selection framework; commit to one figure |
| Day 3 | Unit 3 | Sourcing the Corpus | Read Unit 3 async: Internet Archive, HathiTrust, copyright, OCR quality | Identify and evaluate primary corpus sources for your figure |
| Day 4 | Unit 4 | Text Quality & Preparation | Read Unit 4 async: what makes a good corpus, OCR noise, metadata fields | Assess corpus readiness; identify texts needing remediation |
| Day 5 | Unit 5 | Embedding & AI | Read Unit 5 async: what embeddings are, how RAG retrieval works | Explain the technical architecture in non-technical language |
| Day 6 | Unit 6 | Database Design | Read Unit 6 async: Supabase schema, chunk table structure | Describe the database layer for a funder or administrator |
| Day 7 | Unit 7 | Metadata & Cataloging | Read Unit 7 async: metadata standards, worksMetadata structure, cataloging decisions | Draft works metadata for 5–8 priority corpus items |
| Day 8 | Unit 8 | RAG Pipeline | Read Unit 8 async: how queries flow through the system, what citations mean | Walk a colleague through a full query cycle start to finish |
| Day 9 | Unit 9 | Quality Auditing | Read Unit 9 async: how to evaluate archive answers, what errors look like, how to fix them | Design a quality audit protocol for your archive |
| Day 10 | Workshop | Proposal Writing Workshop | Bring draft corpus inventory and purpose statement | Workshop the proposal document in structured peer-review pairs |
| Day 11 | Workshop | Funder Pitch Preparation | Draft 5-slide pitch deck before this session | Refine pitch with facilitator feedback; practice the 5-minute version |
| Day 12 | Workshop | Final Presentations | Complete proposal and polish pitch deck | Present to the full cohort; receive structured feedback |

## Final Deliverable

**Full Archive Proposal + Funder Pitch Deck**

**Written Proposal** (6–10 pages):
1. Executive summary (1 paragraph)
2. Figure biography and significance
3. Purpose statement and research questions (3 minimum)
4. Corpus inventory with public domain assessment and OCR quality ratings
5. Technical architecture summary (one page, non-technical language)
6. Institutional home, maintenance plan, and succession planning
7. Two-year budget with operating cost projections at 3 traffic scenarios
8. Sustainability model with 5-year view

**Funder Pitch Deck** (5–8 slides):
- Slide 1: The figure and why now
- Slide 2: The research gap this fills
- Slide 3: What the archive can do (live demo or screenshot)
- Slide 4: The team and institutional commitment
- Slide 5: The ask — budget summary and sustainability plan

## Assessment Criteria
- **Proposal completeness**: All eight sections present with specific, verifiable claims
- **Technical accuracy**: Cost estimates, corpus inventory, and architecture summary reflect the curriculum's benchmarks and frameworks
- **Pitch clarity**: The pitch deck communicates the core value proposition in 5 minutes to a non-technical funder
- **Institutional credibility**: The maintenance plan and sustainability model are grounded in named roles and realistic financial commitments
