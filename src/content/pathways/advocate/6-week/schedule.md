# Archive Advocate Pathway — 6-Week Schedule
**Pathway:** Archive Advocate
**Duration:** 6 weeks
**Sessions:** 18 (3 per week, ~75 min each)
**Final Deliverable:** Grant-ready archive brief with 5-year budget and stewardship plan

## Overview
The 6-week advocate pathway is the most comprehensive track, covering all twelve curriculum units and adding six applied sessions on grant budgeting, community partnership design, stewardship planning, stakeholder presentation, peer review, and final presentations. Participants leave with a document ready to submit to an NEH, Mellon, or state humanities council grant program.

## Week-by-Week Schedule

| Day | Unit | Topic | Async Prep | Session Goal |
|-----|------|-------|------------|--------------|
| Day 1 | Unit 1 | Why Living Archives | Read Unit 1 async: what a living archive is, RAG vs. fine-tuning, browse the Du Bois demo | Understand the living archive concept; articulate the case to a non-technical audience |
| Day 2 | Unit 2 | Choosing a Historical Figure | Read Unit 2 async: figure selection criteria, corpus availability, institutional fit | Apply the selection framework; commit to one figure |
| Day 3 | Unit 3 | Sourcing the Corpus | Read Unit 3 async: Internet Archive, HathiTrust, copyright, OCR quality | Identify and evaluate primary corpus sources for your figure |
| Day 4 | Unit 4 | Text Quality & Preparation | Read Unit 4 async: what makes a good corpus, OCR noise, metadata fields | Assess corpus readiness; identify texts needing remediation |
| Day 5 | Unit 5 | Embedding & AI | Read Unit 5 async: what embeddings are, how RAG retrieval works | Explain the technical architecture in non-technical language |
| Day 6 | Unit 6 | Database Design | Read Unit 6 async: Supabase schema, chunk table structure | Describe the database layer for a funder or administrator |
| Day 7 | Unit 7 | Metadata & Cataloging | Read Unit 7 async: metadata standards, worksMetadata structure, cataloging decisions | Draft works metadata for 5–8 priority corpus items |
| Day 8 | Unit 8 | RAG Pipeline | Read Unit 8 async: how queries flow through the system, what citations mean | Walk a colleague through a full query cycle start to finish |
| Day 9 | Unit 9 | Quality Auditing | Read Unit 9 async: how to evaluate archive answers, what errors look like, how to fix them | Design a quality audit protocol for your archive |
| Day 10 | Unit 10 | Cost Modeling & Sustainability | Read Unit 10 async: cost categories, traffic scenarios, sustainability models | Build a cost projection for your archive at 3 traffic scenarios |
| Day 11 | Unit 11 | Deployment & Going Live | Read Unit 11 async: the institutional launch process, usage guidelines, soft launch | Design a soft launch plan with stakeholder communications |
| Day 12 | Unit 12 | Scaling to Community | Read Unit 12 async: community partnerships, HBCU consortia, replication grant language | Draft a community advisory structure and consortium model |
| Day 13 | Applied | Grant Budget Deep-Dive | Bring your cost projection spreadsheet | Workshop the budget narrative: line items, contingencies, indirect costs |
| Day 14 | Applied | Community Partnership Design | Research one HBCU or community org connected to your figure | Design a partnership model with defined roles and decision-making authority |
| Day 15 | Applied | Stewardship Plan Writing | Draft stewardship section before session | Develop a 5-year stewardship plan: who, what, when, how much |
| Day 16 | Applied | Stakeholder Presentation Prep | Draft a 10-minute presentation | Practice and refine the stakeholder pitch with structured peer coaching |
| Day 17 | Applied | Peer Review and Revision | Bring complete draft of all proposal sections | Structured peer review: each participant reviews two others' full proposals |
| Day 18 | Applied | Final Presentations | Final polished proposal and presentation | Present to the full cohort + invited guests; receive structured feedback |

## Final Deliverable

**Grant-Ready Archive Brief with 5-Year Budget and Stewardship Plan** (12–18 pages):

1. Executive summary (1 page)
2. Figure biography and scholarly significance
3. Archive purpose, audience, and 5+ research questions
4. Corpus inventory with full source documentation and public domain assessment
5. Technical architecture summary (non-technical language, suitable for a grant narrative)
6. Community partnership plan: who, what role, how authority is shared
7. Institutional home and succession plan
8. Five-year budget with:
   - Year 1: build costs (labor, infrastructure) + operating costs
   - Years 2–5: operating cost projections at conservative/moderate/ambitious traffic
   - Contingency line for the "went viral" scenario
   - Sustainability model for years 3–5 (endowment, consortium, institutional subsidy)
9. Stewardship plan: corpus update schedule, quality audit cadence, who holds the keys
10. Replication and dissemination plan: how this project enables others to replicate it

**Appendices** (not counted in page limit):
- Full corpus inventory table
- Sample archive interactions (5–8 Q&A pairs demonstrating the archive's capabilities)
- Letters of support from community partners (if available)

## Assessment Criteria
- **Grant readiness**: The brief could be submitted to an NEH Humanities Collections and Reference Resources or Mellon Digital Scholarship grant with minimal additional editing
- **Community grounding**: The partnership plan names specific organizations and proposes a concrete governance structure — not aspirational language
- **Budget defensibility**: Every line item has a specific basis and the 5-year projections use documented cost benchmarks
- **Replication clarity**: The dissemination plan explains how another institution could replicate this work, and the brief could serve as a model for a consortium proposal
